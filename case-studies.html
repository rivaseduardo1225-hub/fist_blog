Case studies

Two field reports — one close to home, one across the Pacific — showing how the same processes of vulnerability play out at different scales.

Local focus · Visayas & Mindanao

Farmers and fisherfolk after Super Typhoon Odette

When Super Typhoon Odette struck in December 2021, coastal and agricultural communities across the Visayas and Mindanao lost both homes and income. Recovery was slow — and the reason was not the storm alone.

Where recovery stalled

  • Smallholder coconut and rice farmers took more than 24 months to return to baseline yields.
  • Fisherfolk rebuilt wooden bangkas using high-interest informal loans.
  • Municipal aid stopped at immediate food relief, without long-term capital replacement.

Recovery was shaped by the capacity gap: limited fiscal space, thin insurance, and aid that addressed immediate needs but not the capital required to rebuild a livelihood. Disaster response and long-term recovery are different problems needing different funding.

Source: Holden et al. (2023)

Global focus · Pacific

Displacement and sovereignty in low-lying atolls

For low-lying Pacific states, sea-level rise is not only an environmental problem. It raises legal and political questions that international law has never had to answer before.

What the research records

  • Saltwater intrusion damages groundwater and traditional taro cultivation.
  • Governments are pursuing "digital statehood" to preserve records and culture.
  • No binding protection exists for citizens who lose sovereign territory.

This case shows displacement as a matter not just of movement but of statehood, culture and legal identity. It connects directly to the protection gap — the absence of an international status for people displaced by climate impacts.

Source: IPCC (2021); Sultana (2022)
